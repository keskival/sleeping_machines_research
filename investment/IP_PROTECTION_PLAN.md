## What the funding should buy

1. **Ownership and disclosure inventory.** Record each candidate invention's
   implementation, technical problem/effect, contributors, dates, prior public
   disclosures and ownership basis. Gather assignment and license documents.
2. **Patentability and commercial review.** Ask patent counsel to assess prior
   art, eligible technical subject matter, inventive step, useful claim scope
   and how a competitor could work around it. Separately scope freedom-to-operate
   work for the first product; patentability is not clearance to commercialize.
3. **Selective priority filings.** File for candidates that pass that review,
   with company ownership established and before further disclosure of their
   unfiled subject matter. Obtain drafting/filing quotes and reserve budget for
   prosecution and justified international extensions. Filing count is not a
   success metric, and a filing is not a granted patent.
4. **Trade-secret and disclosure practice.** Retain confidential implementation
   know-how where appropriate; document access, confidentiality and publication
   decisions. Coordinate patent protection with reproducible investor evidence.

Start the inventory and counsel review in months 0–3. Do not defer protection
of a qualifying invention until all benchmarks or a chip are finished. Later
spending depends on search results, commercial relevance and territory-specific
costs. No fixed number of patent families or grant date is promised.

## Candidate areas to assess, not assertions of novelty

- Temporal/event execution and sparse local-state scheduling with concrete
  computer or device effects.
- Sparse runtime, addressed-memory and communication implementations that
  reduce actual memory traffic or execution overhead.
- Integrated learning/routing implementations and clockless hardware mappings
  where an identifiable technical contribution can be supported.

The broad model-family concept and familiar constituent operators do not by
themselves establish patentability. EPO guidance distinguishes abstract AI/ML
models from inventions contributing a technical solution; novelty and inventive
step remain necessary. [EPO AI/ML examination guidance](https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2026/g_ii_3_3_1.html).

Earlier public disclosure may constrain what remains protectable. Making a
repository private does not erase that history. PRH advises filing before
public disclosure because even the inventor's disclosure can become prior art.
[PRH patent requirements](https://prh.fi/en/intellectualpropertyrights/patentit/theabcofpatenting/whatisapatent.html).

## Investor value and evidence

The rationale is to turn research execution into assets the company can own,
license and defend. Relevant claim coverage, usable rights and implementation
know-how can support defensibility and negotiating value; patent spending alone
does not establish a valuation uplift. Investor diligence should see the rights
inventory, counsel assessment, filing receipts and actual application status,
plus the plan and cost for maintaining protection. Keep confidential technical
details out of unrestricted materials until their disclosure is deliberate.
