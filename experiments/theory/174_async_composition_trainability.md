# 174 — Trainability of asynchronous modules in compositions

Builds on 173 (C1–C9 for compositions of good learners; §14 depth and data; §17 the composing event spine; §19 learning-side
integrations) and 160 (forward/backward duality). Numerical witnesses: `experiments/r1_rmr.py --mode contract`
(`curie_rmr_contracts_20261017_v24`). The propositions are about the construction as implemented; the predictions at the end

## Setting

Module k (a residual-memory level) at step t computes a firing decision φ_kt = 1[σ(f_k(z_kt)) > ½], a correction
o_kt = φ_kt α_k b_k(z_kt) into the shared memory, and an event e_kt = (pointer s*_kt, weight a*_kt), the winner of its read race.
A silent module writes nothing and holds its last event. Module k+1 reads the memory (conditioning path, 173 §16) and module
k's events (pointer and composed pointer candidates, event age, event arrival as a firing input). Hard decisions D (firing,
top-k reads, write addresses, pointers, clock choices) are made identically in training and evaluation; credit to them is
straight-through (forward value 1, gradient through the race probability) plus zero-forward probes for unchosen
alternatives.

## P1. Piecewise exactness

For a fixed pattern of hard decisions D the loss is smooth in the continuous parameters θ, and the forward pass used in
training is the evaluation forward pass. The training gradient is

  g = ∇_θ L |_D + Σ_{d ∈ D} (∂L/∂p_d) ∂p_d/∂θ.

The first term is the exact gradient of the evaluated loss inside the region; the second adds first-order estimates of the
value of each decision through its race probability p_d. So an asynchronous composition is as trainable in its continuous
parameters as a synchronous one with the same hard pattern, and all bias sits in the decision terms. Probes give those terms
credit toward alternatives the forward pass did not take (THEORY P4: credit must reach what did not happen). A soft
relaxation would instead train a different function from the one evaluated (the relaxation gap, DEAD_ENDS C7).

## P2. Silence can be absorbing; gates keep it revivable

If module k never fires on the data (φ_k ≡ 0) and its gates are zero (α_k = 0), every gradient of module k vanishes
through both credit paths, and gradient descent keeps it there: the dead module is a fixed point. If α_k ≠ 0, the
straight-through credit of the firing decision at a silent step is σ'(f) ⟨∂L/∂o, α_k b_k⟩, generically nonzero, so the
module can revive. Contract (v24): with firing bias −10 and zero gates the largest gradient on the level is exactly 0; with
gates 0.5 the firing map receives credit.

Rules that follow: no weight decay on gates or firing biases (matrix-only decay, `--wd-groups 1`); firing starts on
(bias +2); every result reports firing rates per level; an activity cost λ must stay below the marginal revival credit,
otherwise the all-silent state becomes an equilibrium.

## P3. Credit through held events is rate-invariant

A silent module's event at t' is a copy of its event at its last firing τ(t'). The gather that implements the hold routes
every read at t' to τ:

  ∂L/∂e_τ = Σ_{t' : τ(t') = τ} ∂L/∂e_t'.

A module that fires at rate ρ receives a fraction ρ of the credit events, each summing about 1/ρ reads. To first order the
total credit per parameter is the same as at token rate, so sparse firing does not starve learning, provided the held event
is read. Event-driven execution saves forward work without diluting credit.

## P4. Rate matching in a hierarchy

In data generated by a hierarchy with branching s (173 §14), a level-k symbol becomes determinable when its block of s^k
leaves completes. A module that fires at those completions:
- (a) is credited exactly when its decision becomes informative;
- (b) costs about s^-k firings per token, so the total over K levels is a geometric sum bounded independently of K;
- (c) gets durable events. Firing at token rate carries the same information at s^k times the work and adds noise: the
  CERL-H failure (causes changed on 79% of tokens).

Event arrival (the lower module's pointer moved) as a firing input is the mechanism. Prediction: in the synthetic hierarchy
test, firing of level j concentrates after leaves that complete blocks of level ≥ j − 1 (`fire_by_completed_level`, now
recorded by `rhm_depth.py`); in language, firing and event rates fall with level.

## P5. Credit along composed pointers does not vanish with depth

Each event carries a straight-through weight s_i = 1 + p_i − sg(p_i) (forward 1). A composed pointer enters a race with
bonus b ∏_i s_i, and ∂/∂p_i (b ∏_j s_j) = b ∏_{j≠i} s_j = b in the forward pass. Every hop of a chain receives the same credit
whatever its length, while the reach doubles per level (about 2^(K−1) hops for K levels). Soft attention chains instead
multiply probabilities (∏ p_i ≤ 1), and credit decays geometrically with chain length. Straight-through credits the chosen
pointer's weight only; read probes cover the alternatives.

## P6. Bounded delay

Delayed updates (CarryCredit lags H lanes; event-local learners would update on their own schedules) are stale gradients.
For an L-smooth objective, SGD with staleness τ converges at step sizes η ≲ 1/(L(τ + 1)) (bounded-staleness asynchronous
SGD, Lian et al. 2015). With parameter snapshots each delayed gradient is the exact gradient at the parameters that
produced the lane, not a mixture of versions. H = 1 or 2 lanes is negligible staleness at our step sizes.

## P7. State lifetime and credit horizon are separate, and the gap is explicit

Carried memory lives indefinitely; CarryCredit gives exact credit for H lanes (173 §19). A write whose use arrives more
than H lanes later gets no credit for it. Slot clocks and per-correction clocks with horizons much longer than H × 256
tokens need longer credit. For components linear in their writes with exponential clocks (slot memory, old-slot
corrections), forward eligibility traces carry this credit at the cost of one trace per (clock, parameter block)
(theory 160 §1). This is the next learning-side integration.

## P8. Clock races give each stored value a learnable lifetime

With per-correction clocks (`--old-clocks K`), each write step races over K learned rates. The rates receive the exact
gradient of the hard-forward loss (they are continuous), and the choice receives straight-through credit. With all rates
equal, the member reduces exactly to the shared-rate member (contract v23, 5e-7). One shared rate per level forces
corrections that should persist to fade as fast as transient ones; the clock race removes that trade-off.

## Predictions tested by the queued runs

1. Synthetic hierarchy (`curie_rhm_depth_K124_20261017_v1`, native member): K = 4 resolves more depth than K = 1 at the larger
   sample counts, and firing of the upper levels concentrates at block completions (P4).
2. Native 16M (`curie_r1_rmr_nativeev_K4_16M_s0_20261017_v1`): no level is all-silent (P2), and firing and event rates fall
   with level (P4).
3. Cross-lane credit arm (`aws_r1_rmr_nativeev_cc1`): improves the carried and online scores more than the reset-lane
   scores (P7).
4. Per-correction clocks: rates spread over horizons and fix the long-lane regression without the short-context cost of one
   shared rate (P8; arm queued after the decay result).
