# Causal credit when useful inductive biases change

A changing input marginal is different from a changing relationship. Write the conditional future law as P_t(Y|H,a), where a is a permitted intervention on the learner or its route. An inductive bias in credit is an imposed coupling between intervention values across histories, routes or parameter coordinates: equivariance C(T H,T a)=T C(H,a), shared factorization, locality, low rank, or a particular temporal decay. These are properties of a credit function and its intervention domain, not necessarily symmetries of observed inputs alone.

Define the future update objective U_t(a)=E[sum_{h=1}^H w_h loss_{t+h}(theta after a)|H_t]. A useful credit bias compresses U_t or its update derivative across contexts. The forward model supplies observations and paid intervention contrasts; an evolving critic infers which couplings help predict future utility. Neither time reversal nor gradient imitation proves that these couplings are correct. Hidden changes can invalidate an acquired coupling even if the forward marginal frequencies stay constant.

For a local quadratic future objective with positive-semidefinite metric G_t, approximate update error e_t has excess cost e_t^T G_t e_t/2. The relevant approximation norm is therefore determined by future update utility, not an arbitrary cotangent Euclidean norm. This local relation requires its curvature assumption; it is not a global descent guarantee. Exact residual audits can remain unbiased before clipping and nonlinear optimization while an erroneous critic increases update variance and wall time.

A simple tracking calculation separates identifiability from adaptation. If an identifiable expected critic target b_t is observed with unbiased noise xi_t of variance sigma², an exponential update chat_{t+1}=(1-alpha)chat_t+alpha(b_t+xi_t) gives e_{t+1}=(1-alpha)e_t+alpha xi_t-(b_{t+1}-b_t). Under independent zero-mean noise and bounded deterministic drift ||b_{t+1}-b_t||<=v, steady RMS error is at most v/alpha + sqrt(alpha sigma²/(2-alpha)). Choosing alpha balances lag against noise. Unknown context-dependent biases require adequate critic representation and exploration; no step size restores an unidentified intervention target.

The empirical test must hide regime identity, score forecasts before feedback, and share a pre-change actor/critic/optimizer state across controls. Separate frozen forward weights, ordinary online learning, frozen credit with evolving activations, and adapting credit. Report pre-change quality, post-change cumulative excess loss, recovery time, audit costs, critic updates, acquisition and reuse wall time. Controlled conditional changes with preserved marginals identify the mechanism; chronological real-market gains establish usefulness. Neither substitutes for the other. Current B10 chronological continuation is the real-stream development test; changing-bias superiority requires the controlled test and confirmation.
