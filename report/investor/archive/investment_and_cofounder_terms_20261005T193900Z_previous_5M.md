# Sleeping Machines — proposed investment and cofounder terms

**Discussion proposal · 5 October 2026 · EUR**

Sleeping Machines is developing a general-purpose computational substrate spanning language and reasoning, multimodal world models, embodiment, event-native analytics, continual learning, communication, self-design and hardware. Its architecture computes through time and sparse asynchronous events, combining persistent representations with useful capacity beyond active work.

We invite prospective angel investors and cofounders contributing sweat equity to discuss the following investment framework. Participants may contribute cash, work, or both. Cash investment and ongoing founder contribution are valued separately, with both reflected transparently in the final ownership structure.

## 1. Cash investment

| Term | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Pre-money equity valuation | **€5,000,000** |
| Target aggregate new investment | **€250,000–€500,000** |
| Financing basis | A priced equity investment into the company |
| Investor allocation | Proportional to each investor's cash contribution at the agreed share price |
| Use of funds | Founder runway, research and engineering, compute, reproducible validation and initial commercial development |

The €5 million valuation is the proposed value of the company's equity immediately before this financing. New cash increases the post-money valuation.

**Cash investor ownership = investment ÷ (pre-money valuation + total new cash investment).**

The illustrations below assume the indicated amount is the entire round. They exclude additional founder grants, option-pool changes, existing convertible instruments and other simultaneous issuances.

| Total cash raised | Pre-money valuation | Post-money valuation | Aggregate ownership bought with cash |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| €250,000 | €5,000,000 | €5,250,000 | 4.76% |
| €500,000 | €5,000,000 | €5,500,000 | 9.09% |

If several investors participate, they divide the aggregate cash-investor allocation in proportion to their investments. For example, two investors contributing €250,000 each in a €500,000 round each receive approximately **4.55%** from their cash investment alone, under these assumptions.

### Convertible loan alternative

The framework also proposes a **convertible loan** as an alternative to immediate priced equity. Investors provide cash as a loan that converts into shares under agreed conditions. The €250,000–€500,000 funding target may be met through priced equity, convertible loans, or an agreed combination, counting each cash contribution once.

| Term | Proposal for agreement |
|---|---|
| Principal | Each investor's agreed cash contribution |
| Interest | Annual rate, accrual method and whether accrued interest converts with principal to be agreed |
| Maturity | Loan term and maturity date to be agreed |
| Qualified financing | Automatic conversion at a subsequent priced equity round meeting an agreed minimum new-cash threshold |
| Conversion price | Discount to the next round's share price and/or a valuation cap to be agreed; if both apply, use the lower conversion price |
| Valuation cap | Amount and pre-money or post-money basis to be agreed separately from the €5 million priced-equity proposal |
| Conversion shares | Share class and associated rights to be specified |
| No qualifying financing by maturity | Agree repayment, extension by agreement, or conversion under a specified fallback valuation and procedure |
| Sale or change of control before conversion | Agree repayment or conversion treatment and payment priority |
| Security and ranking | Agree whether the loan is unsecured and its ranking relative to other debt |

**Shares issued on conversion = convertible amount ÷ agreed conversion price per share.** The convertible amount includes principal and accrued interest if interest conversion is agreed. Ownership depends on the complete capitalization table at conversion, including new financing and other converting instruments.

The cash-ownership examples above apply to priced equity only. A convertible lender's eventual percentage is determined at conversion. The €5 million priced valuation does not automatically set the loan's valuation cap. Interest, discount, cap, maturity and conversion conditions remain terms for agreement.

## 2. Vesting stock for sweat-equity cofounders

Cofounders joining to contribute work, expertise and long-term commitment may receive a separate allocation of **vesting founder stock (sweat equity)**. A cash investment is not required. A participant contributing both cash and work receives separately documented investment rights and service equity. The allocation will be agreed individually, taking account of:

- The role and responsibility assumed, including technical development, company leadership, commercial development or fundraising.
- Full-time or part-time commitment, start date and expected duration.
- Relevant expertise, execution ability and contribution to building the company.
- Salary arrangements and the personal risk undertaken.

**No additional cofounder percentage is fixed in this proposal.** That percentage requires agreement on the actual role and commitment. Cash investment alone does not determine the additional founder allocation.

The proposed starting point for service equity is **four-year vesting with a one-year cliff**, beginning on the agreed service start date: 25% vests after twelve months of qualifying service, with the remainder vesting monthly over the following thirty-six months. For an illustrative grant of 48,000 shares, 12,000 vest at the cliff and 1,000 vest each month thereafter.

The definitive documents will specify the stock issuance or transfer mechanism, treatment of unvested shares on departure, any company repurchase rights and price, treatment of vested shares, and any acceleration provisions. They will also record the qualifying service commitment and assignment to the company of intellectual property created in the role.

Equity purchased for cash or received on conversion of a cash-funded loan is separate from sweat equity and is not subject to this service-vesting schedule under the proposal.

## 3. Combined ownership and dilution

Each participant's final ownership will reflect their cash-purchased shares, shares received on loan conversion and any agreed sweat-equity allocation. The definitive capitalization table must show:

- Existing ownership before the financing.
- All cash investments and resulting share allocations.
- Outstanding convertible loans, accrued interest, conversion terms and ownership scenarios at conversion.
- Any cofounder service-equity grants, their vesting and their percentage basis.
- Any employee option pool or other reserved equity.
- Fully diluted ownership immediately after all agreed transactions.

Service-equity percentages must specify whether they are calculated before or after the financing and option-pool changes. The cash-only illustrations above are not promises of final combined ownership. Future share issuances may dilute existing shareholders.

## 4. Funding objectives

The initial capital is intended to advance the existing research and implementation into a scalable, reproducibly validated platform. Priorities include integrated model learning and capacity utilization, larger tokenized language comparisons against published Transformer evidence, resource measurement, and selection of an initial commercial application.

The investment case combines the architecture's broad ambition, the implementation and evidence already developed, and the team's ability to execute the next validation and commercialization milestones. Specific technical results and their experimental scope will be supplied in the accompanying research materials.

## 5. Matters to agree before completion

The parties will agree the company's jurisdiction and legal entity, share class and associated rights, governance and decision-making, founder roles and remuneration, service-equity allocations, intellectual-property ownership and licensing, and the final capitalization table. Investment timing and any closing conditions will also be documented.

The initial discussion should establish each participant's proposed cash contribution, financing route, working role, time commitment and availability, then produce one combined capitalization table with conversion scenarios for review. The parties will agree the loan terms listed above if convertible financing is selected.

## 6. Status of this proposal

This document is a **non-binding proposal for discussion**. It is not an offer acceptance, investment commitment or final shareholder agreement. Investment and cofounder arrangements take effect only through the definitive agreements approved and executed by the relevant parties.
